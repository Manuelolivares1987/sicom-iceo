# Shift handover best practices in high-hazard industries: design principles for a handover tool and process

Scope note: applied to a small crew (3 supervisors) on long rotations (7x7). The outgoing supervisor leaves site, so the handover crosses a 7-day gap. Sources are 2010-2026 where possible. The foundational HSE work (Lardner 1992, OTO 96 003) is older but is still the base that all current guidance cites (see Q1). PDF texts were extracted and read in full where noted. Vendor material is flagged as such.

## Q1. What do regulators, industry bodies and incident investigations say about handover failures and requirements?

### Takeaway
All the bodies reviewed (HSE UK, Energy Institute, CCPS, WorkSafe WA, IChemE/Keil) agree on the same core requirements:
- face-to-face, two-way, verbal plus written handover;
- a written procedure that defines the minimum content;
- protected time;
- special treatment of "high-risk handovers", including crew change after a long absence;
- monitoring and auditing.

The major incidents (Piper Alpha, Texas City, Buncefield) all show the same failure mode: a safety-critical status (equipment out of service, a level, a defect) that was not written in the log and not said aloud at handover.

### Cited Findings
**HSE UK guidance**
- HSE UK says handover should be:
  - face-to-face and two-way, with outgoing and incoming staff taking joint responsibility;
  - both verbal and written;
  - based on an analysis of the incoming staff's information needs.

  Outgoing staff should prepare beforehand, and incoming staff should cross-check the information as they take responsibility. Handover should get "as much time and resource as necessary". Organisations should identify higher-risk handovers, provide written procedures and plan maintenance so it is completed within one shift where possible. — [HSE, Shift handover](https://www.hse.gov.uk/humanfactors/topics/shift-handover.htm)
- HSE notes that most accidents involving handover communication failures involved planned maintenance work. It cites the Cullen Report (Piper Alpha) and the 1983 Sellafield beach discharge as handover failures. — [HSE, Shift handover](https://www.hse.gov.uk/humanfactors/topics/shift-handover.htm)
- HSE (OTO 96 003, as quoted by Wilkinson and Lardner) splits a shift changeover into three elements:
  1. preparation by the outgoing person;
  2. the handover exchange itself;
  3. cross-checking by the incoming person as they assume responsibility.

  — [Wilkinson & Lardner, "Pass it on! Revisiting shift handover after Buncefield", IChemE Loss Prevention Bulletin 229, 2013](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)
- HSE lists these as high-risk handovers: after a lengthy absence (crew change or long rest periods); between experienced and inexperienced staff; during an upset; when maintenance spans the handover; and when safety systems are overridden. — [LPB 229, 2013](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)

**Energy Institute research and guidance**
- HSE's national inspection of handover in 16 offshore companies found that some companies:
  - did not define responsibilities and information needs;
  - gave no adequate training or written guidance;
  - did little or no monitoring or auditing of handovers;
  - left handover out of their safety case;
  - had accidents involving miscommunication about maintenance or plant status.

  — [Energy Institute Human Factors Briefing Note 10, Communications (2011), citing HSE OTO 98 160](https://www.hpog.org/assets/documents/BN-10-Communications-web.pdf)
- The Energy Institute says management should ensure that:
  - written guidance defines the key information and how it is exchanged;
  - handovers are not done under time pressure or with distractions;
  - procedures cover higher-risk periods (long absence, overridden safety systems, unusual conditions, unscheduled maintenance, work continuing under an existing permit);
  - handovers are face-to-face wherever possible;
  - tasks are scheduled to finish within a shift;
  - handovers are monitored and audited regularly;
  - the people doing handovers help improve the practice;
  - incidents caused by handover problems are fed back to staff.

  — [EI BN-10](https://www.hpog.org/assets/documents/BN-10-Communications-web.pdf)

**Western Australia regulator**
- WorkSafe WA's 2025 information sheet for major hazard facilities and petroleum operations recommends:
  - a standardised handover procedure (a written shift handover log plus a verbal handover);
  - enough overlap time between shifts;
  - extra time for maintenance or abnormal situations;
  - training in repeat-back and the phonetic alphabet;
  - regular internal assurance audits.

  It says a standardised log "which requires specific information to be communicated, is superior to a general form with generic headings and freehand notes". It also notes that short-term memory holds roughly 5-9 items, so verbal-only handover is limited. — [WorkSafe WA, Human factors: Safety critical communication (2025)](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/system/files/documents/2025-08/hf_safety_critical_communication.pdf)

**Incident investigations**
- Piper Alpha (1988, 167 dead): at shift turnover the status of the pump work was discussed, but the removed relief valve was not mentioned. The relief valve work was also missing from the control-room and maintenance logs. Some workers already knew turnovers and log entries were inadequate. Permits were left on the control room desk instead of being handed personally to the responsible operator. — [CCPS Process Safety Beacon, July 2013](https://www.aiche.org/sites/default/files/2013-07-Beacon-English.pdf)
- Texas City (2005, 15 dead): the CSB report has a dedicated section, "Inadequate Shift Turnover" (2.5.4). — [CSB Final Report 2005-04-I-TX](https://www.csb.gov/assets/1/20/CSBFinalReportBP.pdf?13841)
  - An ASM Consortium paper lists "a failure to log information and an informal and unstructured shift handover process" as contributing root causes. — [Plocher et al., Effective Shift Handover, ASM Consortium/Honeywell](https://process.honeywell.com/content/dam/process/en/documents/document-lists/doc_asm-consortium/white-papers/July%2031%202011%20-%20Effective%20Shift%20Handover.pdf)
  - Secondary summaries add three details: the night operator left about an hour early with only a brief update; the splitter tower level was not in the logbook; and BP had no shift-turnover communication requirement. These are not verified against the CSB text. — [BARPI summary](https://www.aria.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/wp-content/files_mf/A29598_ips29598_001.pdf); [Brazier blog](https://andybrazier.blogspot.com/2007/03)
- API RP 755 (fatigue risk management for refining and petrochemical) was written in response to a 2007 CSB recommendation after Texas City. It sets hours-of-service limits and requires documented risk assessment for exceptions. — [API RP 755 fact sheet](https://API.org/-/media/Files/Oil-and-Natural-Gas/Refining/Process%20Safety/RP-755-Fact-Sheet.pdf); [API RP 755 page](https://www.api.org/oil-and-natural-gas/health-and-safety/refinery-and-plant-safety/process-safety/process-safety-standards/rp-755)
- Buncefield (2005): the initial confusion for both supervisors arose at end of shift and handover, and handover instructions lacked detail. The Tank 912 level transmitter had flat-lined at least 14 times in the previous 3 months without effective action. Secondary analyses link "defects not communicated" to handover. — [humanfactors101, Buncefield](https://humanfactors101.com/incidents/buncefield-11-december-2005/); [Elliott, CAST analysis of Buncefield (ZHAW 2016)](https://www.zhaw.ch/storage/engineering/institute-zentren/iamp/sp_sks/ESW2016/19-Elliott-CASTAnalysisOfTheBuncefieldIncident.pptx.pdf)
- The Energy Institute names poor communication, especially at handover, as a key contributor to Piper Alpha, Grangemouth, Texas City and Buncefield. — [EI BN-10](https://www.hpog.org/assets/documents/BN-10-Communications-web.pdf)

**Industry data and expert reviews**
- An ADNOC/SPE study (SPE 202862-MS) reports two findings:
  - Baseline reviews found that 80% of facility logbooks were unstructured and did not capture key information.
  - One operator's research found that startup, shutdown and changeover take under 5% of operations time but account for 40% of plant incidents.

  — [JPT, "Process safety and operational integrity rely on effective shift handovers"](https://jpt.spe.org/process-safety-and-operational-integrity-rely-on-effective-shift-handovers)
- Wilkinson and Lardner (Keil Centre, 2013) conclude that the gap is in applying existing knowledge, not in the research. They also say the focus on shift-to-shift handover is too narrow: within-shift and shift-to-management communication matter just as much. — [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)

### Inferences
- The failures repeat a single pattern: open or abnormal states that never reach the incoming person. Examples are equipment out of service, ongoing maintenance, overridden protections and recurring defects. A tool should therefore treat open items as persistent objects that carry forward until someone explicitly closes them. They should not be free text inside a daily note.
- "No monitoring or auditing" is a named failure in HSE's own inspection, so the tool has to produce an audit trail as a by-product (who handed over, who accepted, when, what was open).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve primary text from IOGP or ICMM on handover. ICMM guidance appears to cover the topic only within broader critical-control/fatality-prevention material. I found no dedicated IOGP handover report.
- The API RP 755 text is paywalled. I found no authoritative statement of its specific handover or overlap provisions; the only mention was a vendor blog citing "14 h including handover", which is unverified.
- I could not open the CSB text of section 2.5.4 directly, so the Texas City details come from secondary sources.

## Q2. Structured protocols and their evidence: SBAR, I-PASS, verbal + written, read-back, walk-around, overlap, checklist, prioritisation, acknowledgement, open actions, accountability

### Takeaway
The strongest outcome evidence is from healthcare. In I-PASS, medical errors fell 23% and preventable adverse events fell 30% (pre-post design, 9 hospitals). In process industry, a controlled experiment found that a checklist-structured log captured 18.6 points more of the expected items than a less-structured log, at a cost of about 44 seconds of extra handover time. The design elements that recur everywhere are:
- a fixed minimum checklist, with "nothing to report" acknowledged explicitly;
- a severity or priority flag;
- an action list with an owner;
- if-then contingencies;
- a receiver read-back or synthesis;
- face-to-face exchange backed by written records;
- protected time of up to about 30 minutes for a 12-hour shift.

### Cited Findings
**I-PASS (healthcare)**
- I-PASS (Starmer et al., NEJM 2014;371:1803-12) was a multicentre pre-post study in 9 pediatric hospitals with 10,740 admissions:
  - Medical errors fell from 24.5 to 18.8 per 100 admissions (−23%).
  - Preventable adverse events fell from 4.7 to 3.3 per 100 admissions (−30%).
  - Communication improved with no negative effect on workflow.
  - Because the design is pre-post, it does not prove causation.

  — [ScienceDaily / NEJM press release](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/11/141105203533.htm); [Intermountain release](https://intermountainhealthcare.org/news/2014/12/multicenter-patientsafety-study-reduces-medical-error-injuries-by-30); [The Hospitalist synopsis](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/resident-handoff-program-associated-improved-inpatient-outcomes)
- The I-PASS components are:
  - **I**llness severity: a one-word rating, "stable / watcher / unstable".
  - **P**atient summary.
  - **A**ction list: a to-do list with timing and ownership; if there is nothing to do, it says "nothing to do".
  - **S**ituation awareness and contingency planning, in "if-then" form.
  - **S**ynthesis by receiver: the receiver summarises, asks questions and restates the key actions.

  — [AHRQ PSNet, Handoffs primer](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/handoffs); [CASRAI I-PASS guide](https://casrai.org/guides/ipass-handoff)
- Read-back by the receiver, followed by time for questions, is how I-PASS confirms comprehension. Outside I-PASS, read-back is uncommon in healthcare except for verbal or phone orders. — [search summary of handoff review literature; primary not opened](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9923540)
- The Joint Commission does not endorse a specific tool. It expects handoffs to cover illness assessment, a summary, a to-do list and contingency plans. — [CASRAI I-PASS guide](https://casrai.org/guides/ipass-handoff)

**Structured log experiment (process industry)**
- The ASM Consortium ran a controlled experiment at the ENGEN refinery in Durban: 10 operator pairs on a high-fidelity simulator.
  - **Design:** a checklist-integrated logbook against a less-structured log with generic headings. In the checklist version, if a section did not apply, the operator had to acknowledge that there was nothing to report.
  - **Logbook completeness:** 76.1% vs 57.5% of expected items (+18.6 points, p<0.02).
  - **Recall by the incoming operator:** 51.1% vs 42.1% (+9 points, p=0.12, a trend).
  - **Correct answers to probe questions:** 56.4% vs 48.6% (+7.9 points, p=0.11).
  - **Duration:** 324 s vs 280 s (+16%, not significant).

  — [Plocher et al., ASM Consortium/Honeywell](https://process.honeywell.com/content/dam/process/en/documents/document-lists/doc_asm-consortium/white-papers/July%2031%202011%20-%20Effective%20Shift%20Handover.pdf)

**Face-to-face plus written, from aviation and nuclear**
- The same paper summarises aviation maintenance research (Parke & Kanki):
  - Face-to-face handovers are essential and work better with structured written support.
  - Written material adds redundancy.
  - A checklist forces the organisation to decide in advance which items must never be left out.
  - Face-to-face with written support beat written-only handovers where verbal communication was filtered through a supervisor.
  - Face-to-face turnover with written support is standard in US nuclear plants.

  — [Plocher et al.](https://process.honeywell.com/content/dam/process/en/documents/document-lists/doc_asm-consortium/white-papers/July%2031%202011%20-%20Effective%20Shift%20Handover.pdf)

**Verbal-only handover loses information (not verified against primary)**
- Pothier et al. (2005), a simulation, reportedly found zero data retained with verbal-only handover, 31% with verbal plus note-taking, and minimal loss with a typed handover sheet. This comes from search summaries; I did not open the primary paper. — [summary via PMC table (not opened, captcha)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3575739/table/T1)

**Time, preparation and good-practice lists (process safety)**
- Management should set a minimum handover period. On a 12-hour shift a handover typically lasts up to 30 minutes, and less if it is well structured. Other recommendations:
  - pay for or reward the extra time;
  - allow time before to prepare and time after to consolidate and check;
  - postpone key tasks and minimise interruptions during changeover;
  - repeat key information in more than one medium;
  - have the people involved actively confirm and clarify;
  - "minimise unnecessary information so that the system does not become devalued".

  — [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)
- Lardner's 1999 review of an offshore company listed these elements of good practice:
  - well-designed logs and job aids;
  - checklists or prompts;
  - redundancy and diversity of media, with two-way feedback;
  - a specified expected handover time;
  - analysis of information needs for each operating mode (normal, abnormal, start-up/shutdown, maintenance), based on hazard;
  - a reading file for crew changeovers or after long rest periods;
  - group handovers as well as individual ones.

  — [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)
- The handover content must include what is needed to control and prevent major accidents, not just occupational safety items. — [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)
- A UK nuclear example combined one-to-one handovers with a brief (about 15 minutes) stand-up group handover led by the incoming shift manager. The reviewers still judged it short of good practice because it did not identify higher-risk handovers. — [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)
- Air traffic control uses a content mnemonic (PRAWNS) built from an analysis of the key information. Mnemonics help people recall what must be recorded and said. — [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)

**Applied industry cases**
- A Keil Centre refinery project developed a behaviour standard for conducting handovers plus structured shift logs, with wide workforce consultation. It produced significant improvement in what was logged and in how handovers were conducted. — [EI BN-10, case study 2](https://www.hpog.org/assets/documents/BN-10-Communications-web.pdf); [Lardner, Safe communication at shift handover (IChemE)](https://www.icheme.org/media/25354/major-hazards-onshore-and-offshore-ii-icheme-symposium-series-139-1995-30-adamson.pdf)
  - A secondary source cites a 71% improvement in log content and a 66% improvement in the handover process; I could not verify these figures. — [Yokogawa blog](https://blog.yokogawa.com/blog/ensuring-quality-shift-handover)
- CCPS (Piper Alpha lesson): document the status of all equipment and all maintenance jobs, and take time at end of shift to make sure the incoming person fully understands. Hand permits over personally; do not leave them on a desk. — [CCPS Beacon July 2013](https://www.aiche.org/sites/default/files/2013-07-Beacon-English.pdf)

### Inferences
These design principles for the tool follow from the evidence:
1. **Fixed minimum checklist.** Each section must be answered, either with content or with an explicit "nothing to report". No blank free-text form.
2. **Severity/priority flag per item.** Use a three-level scale like I-PASS ("normal / watch / critical"), with safety-critical and major-hazard items shown first.
3. **Action list with owner and due date.** Items persist and carry forward until closed; the incoming supervisor must accept them.
4. **If-then contingencies** for each watched item.
5. **Receiver synthesis or acknowledgement.** The incoming supervisor confirms in the tool and can add questions; the handover is not "closed" until accepted.
6. **Written first, then live conversation.** Written alone is weaker than written plus voice; a call is acceptable when the two cannot meet in person.
7. **Protected time:** up to about 30 minutes, at a fixed hour.
8. **Brevity.** Avoid noise that devalues the system.

On applicability to 3 supervisors: the per-handover cost is small (about 5-30 minutes). With few people, handover quality depends strongly on each individual's communication skill. That makes a standard structure more valuable, not less (see the SH2 finding in Q3).

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed outcome trial of SBAR in industrial settings. SBAR evidence is mainly from healthcare, and I did not retrieve SBAR-specific outcome data in this pass.
- I found no published walk-around or at-the-workplace handover evidence for industry. The healthcare analogue (bedside handover; Cochrane CD009979) could not be retrieved (HTTP 403).

## Q3. Long rotations (7x7, FIFO, 14/21-day tours): continuity across the gap, crew-to-crew handover, remote communication

### Takeaway
Crew change after days off is explicitly a high-risk handover in HSE guidance. Two things happen at once: the incoming person's awareness has decayed over the rest period, and the outgoing person is at their most fatigued. The recommended countermeasures are:
- a "reading file" or catch-up record of what happened in the intervening period;
- a formal handover that does not depend on a quick chat at the airport or transport point;
- persistent open items.

There is almost no peer-reviewed research quantifying information loss specifically at rotation changeovers.

### Cited Findings
**Offshore tours and crew change**
- Offshore crews typically have 2-3 weeks off between tours. This puts "significant additional emphasis on a good quality handover, given the sizeable decay in awareness" over the rest period.
  - The outgoing person is likely at their most fatigued.
  - Transport and communication difficulties make consistent handovers hard.
  - Anecdotally, companies rely too much on quick face-to-face conversations in the heliport lounge to supplement formal changeover.
  - Higher-risk handovers are not consistently identified.

  — [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)
- Longer periods off between cycles require "more effort and time ... to bring returning staff up-to-speed" and increase reliance on logs and written communication. — [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)
- Handovers between the same two people can let mistakes persist unchallenged. If the outgoing person briefs incorrectly and later takes over again, the wrong picture can survive. Shared assumptions can also breed complacency. — [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)
- Use a "reading file for crew changeovers or after long rest periods so new crew can catch up fully with what has occurred in the intervening period". — [LPB 229 (Lardner 1999)](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)

**Remote sites**
- Offshore working is effectively a complete shift operation because of the physical separation from shore. Asset-to-shore communication is "very vulnerable to disruption and misunderstanding". Some companies use video/audio links and head-mounted cameras. — [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)

**Handover scope beyond shift-to-shift**
- The Energy Institute says information must reach everyone who needs it, explicitly including mobile teams and "crews that change their membership from time to time". It lists "after long periods of employee absence" as a higher-risk handover period. — [EI BN-10](https://www.hpog.org/assets/documents/BN-10-Communications-web.pdf)
- HSE defines the critical communication times as: shift changeover, between shift and day workers, between functions within a shift (operations and maintenance), and during upsets. For remote sites, communication with the base ("beach") is added. — [LPB 229 quoting HSE](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)

**Mining (vendor source)**
- The SH2 mining project (Sedgman, 5D Consulting, Queensland) found that handovers in all industries are "generally inconsistent except where supervisors have excellent communication skills". A structured, visual process makes quality less dependent on the supervisor's presentation skills.
  - Features: per-person sign-in and traceability; attendance tracking; items pinned to a map location; offline use with sync; and a communications section so people off site can get messages into the handover.
  - Reported results: −30% reportable injury frequency at Sedgman within 3 months; at BGC, +70% hazard observations, −48% incidents and about 60 minutes of supervisor time freed.
  - Caveat: this is a vendor whitepaper with no independent verification.

  — [Weule, SH2 whitepaper, QMIHSC 2016](https://www.qmihsconference.org.au/wp-content/uploads/qmihsc-2016-writtenpaper-weule.pdf)

**Research on long tours**
- The Norwegian assessment of extended offshore stays (STAMI, for Havtil) states that the authors know of no literature on how stays beyond 14 days affect accidents or health. It notes that offshore workers need at least 5 days to recover. — [STAMI 2021 for Havtil](https://www.havtil.no/contentassets/73be2ed14eb14997ac71d145e28d523a/2021-stami-helse--og-sikkerhetsrisiko.-vurdering-av-forlengede-oppholdsperioder-offshore-og-konsekvenses-for-ansattes-helseulyk.pdf)

### Inferences
For a 7x7 crew of 3 supervisors:
- **Two handover types.** The tool should keep the daily handover separate from the rotation handover. Daily handovers happen within a rotation or between day and night shifts, if that applies. The rotation handover (crew A to crew B) is higher risk and needs more content and more time.
- **Automatic 7-day "reading file".** Compile the open items, events, equipment status, changes, incidents and pending actions since the incoming supervisor last left. Generate it from the daily records so nobody has to rewrite it.
- **Voice exchange that cannot be skipped.** When the outgoing supervisor has already left site, require a scheduled call or video call with acknowledgement recorded in the tool. A chat at the bus or airport should not count.
- **Item ownership moves at changeover.** The outgoing supervisor cannot "close" the rotation with unassigned open items, and the incoming supervisor's acceptance transfers ownership.
- **A third-person check.** With only 3 supervisors, the same pair hands over to each other repeatedly, so a wrong shared picture can persist. A periodic check by someone other than the pair, such as a manager reviewing open items, guards against this.
- **Offline capture.** Field sites with patchy coverage need offline entry with sync, as SH2 offered.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study that quantifies information loss or incident rates at 7x7 or FIFO rotation changeovers. This is a real gap in the literature, confirmed by the Norwegian report.
- I found no Chilean regulatory requirement (Sernageomin / DS 132) on shift handover; it was not researched in this pass.

## Q4. Measurable indicators of handover quality and how to audit them

### Takeaway
The audit methods are sample-based. Auditors check that the handover documentation is complete, judge the quality of the spoken handover, confirm that the incoming team accepted it, and test what the incoming person actually understands. Logs are scored against an expert "model log" of the items that should be there, and incoming staff are asked probe questions. Lagging indicators are counts of handover-related problems and incidents.

### Cited Findings
- The Energy Institute's sample KPIs:
  - % of shift handovers meeting required criteria, and the number of errors found (from sample audits of the handover and review of logs);
  - accuracy and usefulness of shift logs;
  - number of reported end-of-tour or handover problems;
  - % compliance with communication protocols (spot checks);
  - correct use of proformas.

  The criteria checks should cover "correct completion of handover documentation, quality of spoken handover, and acceptance of handover by incoming team". — [EI BN-10](https://www.hpog.org/assets/documents/BN-10-Communications-web.pdf)
- The EI warning-sign questions include: "Are shift handovers very often rushed with the minimum of information exchanged?" and "Could work be better planned ... so that there is no need to hand over a job?" — [EI BN-10](https://www.hpog.org/assets/documents/BN-10-Communications-web.pdf)
- The ASM experiment used three quality measures and a duration measure:
  1. Log quality: % of the items in an expert-written model log that were actually logged.
  2. Incoming operator recall: % of key items recalled immediately after handover.
  3. % of correct answers to probe questions during the shift.
  4. Handover duration.

  — [Plocher et al.](https://process.honeywell.com/content/dam/process/en/documents/document-lists/doc_asm-consortium/white-papers/July%2031%202011%20-%20Effective%20Shift%20Handover.pdf)
- HSE links to a methodology for characterising the type and quality of handover activities and improving current practice. An HSE audit method was also developed for offshore use. — [HSE Shift handover](https://www.hse.gov.uk/humanfactors/topics/shift-handover.htm); [LPB 229](https://www.icheme.org/media/2309/lpb229_pp25.pdf)
- WorkSafe WA calls for regular internal assurance audits to confirm handover controls are effective and maintained. — [WorkSafe WA 2025](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/system/files/documents/2025-08/hf_safety_critical_communication.pdf)
- SH2 tracks attendance at handover or pre-start and per-user traceability of changes as compliance evidence. (Vendor source.) — [SH2 whitepaper](https://www.qmihsconference.org.au/wp-content/uploads/qmihsc-2016-writtenpaper-weule.pdf)

### Inferences
Indicators the tool can compute automatically:
- % of handovers completed on time;
- % accepted (acknowledged) by the incoming supervisor, and the time to acknowledgement;
- % with all mandatory sections answered;
- number of open items carried forward and their age (days open);
- items closed without evidence;
- % of rotation handovers that had a recorded voice or video session.

Lagging indicators to track:
- incidents or near misses where "information not passed on at handover" is a cause;
- the number of times the incoming supervisor found an undocumented condition.

A manual audit step, about monthly for a crew this size: a manager picks one handover, writes the "model" list of what should have been there, and compares. They also ask the incoming supervisor 3-5 probe questions about the inherited situation.

### Gaps
- I found no published benchmark targets, such as an acceptable % completeness or duration, for industrial handover KPIs.
- I could not retrieve the HSE audit methodology documents (OTO 98 160 and "Improving communication at shift handover") in full text.
