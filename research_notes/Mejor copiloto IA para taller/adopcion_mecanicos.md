# Adoption of AI/digital assistants by mechanics and field technicians: why they succeed or fail, and which UX patterns drive daily use (2023-2026)

Context: in-house workshop copilot (Chilean heavy-truck fleet, ~10 workshop mechanics plus field mechanics at mines; PWA chat inside the work-order app; Spanish; shared tablet accounts; noise, dirty hands, gloves). Current usage: ~27 queries/month, 0 diagnostic cases registered, 0 thumbs up/down; an earlier version asked for 3 forms for the learning loop and got zero use.

Overall evidence quality: rigorous independent studies of AI copilots for *mechanics* are scarce. The best peer-reviewed evidence comes from (a) factory operators using LLM knowledge assistants (European detergent plants, arXiv 2024), and (b) the Brynjolfsson et al. NBER study of support agents. Most field-service numbers (Aquant, Neuron7, Geotab, BCG, McKinsey) are vendor or consultancy claims without control groups. I found no documented Latin American case of an AI assistant for mechanics; Chilean/Peruvian mining AI evidence is about predictive maintenance.

## 1. Documented reasons technicians reject AI tools, and what fixed them

### Takeaway
Rejection comes less from the model than from the workflow: tools that sit outside the job, demand extra effort, give answers that are wrong or out of date in a safety-critical setting, or feel like surveillance get quietly dropped. Workers also prefer asking a nearby expert when one is available. What helped: framing the assistant as support (not a decision-maker), expert-approved content with sources, minimal-effort interaction, local language, company-provided devices, and visible leadership support.

### Cited Findings
- Two-year longitudinal study (4 phases, 251 comments, N=40 operators and managers, two European factories) of an LLM cognitive assistant for knowledge sharing. Operators wanted **minimal-effort interactions**, while management wanted detailed, standardized records. This is the same split behind SICOM's failed "3 forms" learning loop. — [Factory Operators' Perspectives on Cognitive Assistants (arXiv 2409.20192)](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20192v1)
- Same study: some operators resented management's implication that they were hoarding knowledge ("accused of not sharing"). Operators were more willing to use or share knowledge **when production was stopped by a critical problem**. Most supported using the assistant as a **first escalation step before calling busy colleagues**. It was unclear whether they would keep up the effort over time. — [arXiv 2409.20192](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20192v1)
- Surveillance: one operator refused to take part, citing discomfort with tracking and covert oversight, and another covered a camera with a sticker. The authors warn that operators may not feel safe to oppose tools openly, so silence is not consent. — [arXiv 2409.20192](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20192v1)
- Trust and accuracy: safety was the main worry ("inadequate responses risk safety in a hazardous industry"). Participants also raised accountability for mistakes and worried that entries would become **outdated or conflicting**. Suggested safeguards were expert approval of entries, conflict checks, outdated-content detection, and framing the tool as support, not decision-maker ("useful as a supportive tool but not a solution", P34). — [arXiv 2409.20192](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20192v1)
- History of failed tools: "We bought a bunch of tablets 7 years ago with the intention of using them for documentation, but they were never adopted" (P23). On upkeep: "The biggest challenge is keeping it updated. It takes so many man-hours, and you just don't have those" (P24). — [arXiv 2409.20192](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20192v1)
- Operator and manager recommendations: short training or demos, keyword recognition and autocomplete, local-language versions, **company-provided devices** (personal phones were a concern), reliable Wi-Fi, involving operators in the rollout, and showing clear benefits with visible management investment. — [arXiv 2409.20192](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20192v1)
- Field user study of an LLM factory assistant (9 participants: 4 managers, 5 operators). Users valued "having information at hand", but speed was lost when the problem was not in the system's history (wasted time). Some said **it is faster to ask a nearby colleague** (P8). The authors position the tool for when no expert is around, such as night shifts with a single operator. Recommendations: let users open the full source document (PDF), clearly signal when there is no reliable answer, keep content specific and current, and seek buy-in including older operators. — [Fabrication-floor LLM knowledge study (arXiv 2401.05200)](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.05200v2)
- Same study's benchmark (20 questions, half from manuals and half from issue reports): GPT-4 scored 97.5 factuality with 0 hallucinations; Llama 2 hallucinated 13% and Guanaco 65%. Model choice strongly affects trust-destroying errors. — [arXiv 2401.05200](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.05200v2)
- BCG AI at Work 2025 (10,600+ workers, 11 countries): frontline regular GenAI use has **stalled at 51%** (a "silicon ceiling"), versus over three-quarters of managers and leaders. Only about one quarter of frontline staff report strong leadership support. With strong support, the share who feel positive about GenAI rises from **15% to 55%**. Only one third say they were properly trained. — [BCG, AI at Work 2025](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/ai-at-work-momentum-builds-but-gaps-remain)
- BCG: top concerns are decisions without human oversight (46%), unclear accountability for mistakes (35%), and bias or unfair treatment (32%). Secondary coverage, not verified against the primary report. — [Unleash summary of BCG 2025](https://www.unleash.ai/unleashworld/hr-tech/bcgs-ai-at-work-2025-report-four-takeaways-for-hr-leaders)
- MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide 2025" (150 interviews, 350-employee survey, 300 deployments): most enterprise pilots stall because tools "don't learn from or adapt to workflows" (a learning gap), not because of model quality. Vendor or partnership solutions reportedly succeed ~67% of the time, and internal builds about one third as often. The "95% fail" figure refers to rapid revenue impact, and coverage varies on what it means. — [Fortune coverage](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/?mod=article_inline); [Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/mit-enterprise-ai-pilots-fail-revenues)
- Chilean mining: the main difficulty in AI implementation is change management and the skepticism new tools trigger. Vantaz Analytics (Jan 2026) proposes GenAI as a "first level of diagnosis and technical consultation" that combines failure history, manuals, alarms and records, but warns it needs **source control, traceability and answer validation**. This is a proposal, not a documented deployment. — [Reporte Minero, Vantaz 2026](https://www.reporteminero.cl/noticia/reportajes/2026/01/inteligencia-artificial-mineria-valor-actual-desafios-vantaz-analytics); [Portal Minero](https://www.portalminero.com/las-mineras-van-a-producir-sus-propias-ia-la-irrupcion-de-los-agentes-en-las-faenas-chilenas)
- Vendor and practitioner guidance (opinion, not evidence): adoption in maintenance teams is "primarily a people challenge". Choose respected technicians as champions, not just early adopters. Show the source of each answer (manual page or past work order). — [MaintainX](https://www.getmaintainx.com/blog/practical-strategies-to-help-your-maintenance-team-embrace-ai); [LLumin](https://llumin.com/blog/how-to-build-technician-trust-in-ai-powered-alerts/)

### Inferences
- The SICOM numbers (27 queries, 0 cases, 0 thumbs) fit the documented pattern. A pull-style chat outside the moment of need competes with "ask Rodrigo or the colleague next to me", and loses whenever an expert is nearby. The highest-value moments are when no expert is around: field mechanics at mine sites, night shifts, and a stopped truck with an unknown fault.
- A shared tablet account plus a learning loop that looks like "management wants records" can read as surveillance or extra paperwork. Messaging matters: the assistant is there to help the mechanic, and any knowledge credit goes to him by name.
- A single wrong or generic answer early on is very costly in a safety-critical, skeptical crowd. "I don't know, ask X" plus source links is better than a confident generic answer.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study specifically on heavy-equipment mechanics' rejection reasons for LLM copilots was found. The best evidence is from factory operators.
- No quantified data on how "management surveillance" fears depress use of AI chat (as opposed to cameras) in workshops.
- The primary BCG and MIT reports were not read in full; some figures come from secondary coverage.

## 2. UX patterns that drive daily use

### Takeaway
The patterns with the most support are: embed in the work order and be proactive (asset history, "this happened before"), minimal input (keyword or quick-pick plus photo, rather than typing), answers short with the source page attached, and local language and jargon. Voice is promoted heavily by vendors (Cat, Aquant), but the only independent factory study found voice unreliable in noise and with jargon, and dropped it. Treat voice as optional and test it on site; don't make it the main channel.

### Cited Findings
- **Voice, mixed evidence.** In the factory longitudinal study, voice was unreliable because of factory noise and was **dropped for text-only** in phase 2, partly because of noise and jargon. Headsets were uncomfortable over long periods. Interactions needed to be shorter. Participants suggested **pictures or pictograms** for mechanical problems. — [arXiv 2409.20192](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20192v1)
- **Voice, vendor direction.** The Cat AI Assistant (CES 2026) lets technicians use voice commands to pull up repair manuals, get **step-by-step repair guidance** and find parts. Speech recognition runs at the machine edge (NVIDIA Jetson Thor). It is built on the Helios platform (16+ PB of equipment data). No adoption metrics were published. — [Caterpillar press release](https://www.caterpillar.com/content/caterpillarDotCom/en/news/corporate-press-releases/h/cat-ai-assistant.html); [OEM Off-Highway](https://www.oemoffhighway.com/electronics/smart-systems/product/22957823/caterpillar-inc-caterpillar-launches-ai-assistant-for-equipment-management); [Recycling Today](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/caterpillar-introduces-cat-ai-assistant)
- Aquant "Roger" is a voice agent that answers technicians and captures expert knowledge, designed for low connectivity. Vision AI is in early access. Vendor material only. — [Aquant](https://www.aquant.ai/de/solutions/scale-service-teams)
- **Show the source.** Users want to open the full source document, not just a list of documents. Signal clearly when no reliable answer exists. — [arXiv 2401.05200](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.05200v2). Factory study: display attachments after the main text, and add keyword recognition and autocomplete. — [arXiv 2409.20192](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20192v1)
- **Asset- and history-aware, proactive.** BCG's 2026 field-service perspective describes the shift from the 2023 "chatbot queries asset history and manuals" mode to agents that "proactively flag potential asset performance issues", "recommend troubleshooting steps and required parts" and "summarize past incidents to support troubleshooting". It reports GenAI technician copilots at **+5-10% daily technician productivity** (BCG project claim). It says copilots must deliver insights "directly within existing workflows" and integrate with existing systems "ensuring minimal disruption". — [BCG Executive Perspectives: Future of Field Service with Applied AI (June 2026)](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/executive-perspectives-future-of-field-service-with-applied-ai.pdf)
- BCG XR or hands-free example (maintenance and operations): -40% errors and rework, +25% faster maintenance execution. Change management (continuous communication, training, integrations) is listed as one of four key elements. These are BCG claims. — [BCG 2026 PDF](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/executive-perspectives-future-of-field-service-with-applied-ai.pdf)
- Ascendum (Portuguese equipment dealer, Volvo CE distributor) with McKinsey/QuantumBlack: a GenAI search over **13,000+ technical documents** was embedded inside the existing Salesforce Service Cloud workflow. The pilot was built in 4 weeks. It reportedly improved first-time resolution and diagnosis accuracy, and reduced downtime. No percentages were published. — [McKinsey case study](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/case-studies/adding-a-powerful-new-tool-to-the-field-mechanics-toolbelt-ai) (page timed out; details from search snippets); [McKinsey, From pilot to profit](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/industrials/our-insights/from-pilot-to-profit-scaling-gen-ai-in-aftermarket-and-field-services)
- Automotive: Mastertech.ai combines OEM repair info, vehicle history and the **shop's own repair records**. Mykaarma's Pre-Diag (an AI customer questionnaire before the technician starts) reportedly saved ~40 min per diagnostic repair order across the first 40 dealerships. Vendor claims with no control group. — [AutoVitals Mastertech](https://www.autovitals.com/mastertech/); [Mykaarma](https://mykaarma.com/news/ai-powered-customer-quiz-streamlines-dealership-diagnostics-before-technicians-start-work/)
- **One-tap feedback rarely works on its own.** Explicit feedback in production LLM apps runs about 1-3% of interactions (practitioner estimate). An Amazon production chatbot reported under 5% of responses get any feedback. LangChain's Chat-LangChain got ~0.04% explicit feedback, while ~70% of queries were follow-ups that implicitly signal answer quality. Thumbs ratings correlate weakly with real quality. — [LangChain chat-bot feedback](https://python.langchain.com/docs/templates/chat-bot-feedback); [Amazon Science paper](https://assets.amazon.science/32/ce/33b9c3994947814c39da3995151c/scipub-approval152129-45631012-from-metrics-to-meaning-estimating-user-feedback-using-llmbased-evaluation.pdf); [tianpan.co (practitioner)](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-20-behavioral-signals-ai-user-satisfaction); [Feedback by Design, arXiv 2602.01405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.01405)

### Inferences
- Zero thumbs up or down out of 27 queries is the expected base rate (1-3% of 27 is less than 1). It is not by itself a sign of dissatisfaction. The learning signal should come from implicit behavior: the reformulated question, the follow-up, the opened manual page, and above all what was actually done to the OT at close.
- For SICOM, the strongest pattern is **push inside the OT**. When an OT or NC opens for a patente, show a card like "Este equipo tuvo X en <fecha>, se resolvió con Y (OT #)" plus the pauta or manual page for that model. The mechanic gets value with zero typing, and the copilot's credibility is built on their own fleet's history, not generic internet knowledge.
- Input should be photo-first with quick picks (sistema, síntoma, código de falla) and optional dictation. Free typing with gloves is the worst case. Voice transcription should be field-tested in the Coquimbo workshop and at mine sites, with Chilean jargon, before relying on it.
- Answers: a short numbered step list in Chilean workshop Spanish, the source (manual page or diagram image, or past OT) attached under the text, and an explicit "no tengo esto documentado, consulta a Rodrigo" when there is no grounding.

### Gaps
- No independent measurement found of voice-recognition accuracy in heavy-vehicle workshops, or of Spanish (Chilean) jargon performance.
- No published A/B evidence comparing proactive in-work-order suggestions with pull chat for technicians. The support is consultancy framing plus HCI recommendations.
- Glove or dirty-hand interaction studies for LLM assistants were not found.

## 3. Knowledge capture without extra work

### Takeaway
Capture should happen as a by-product of closing the OT: dictated or photo notes turned into a structured summary by AI, which a mechanic or jefe confirms with one tap, not separate forms. Expert curation (approval before content becomes "official") is what operators themselves asked for, to keep trust and avoid stale or conflicting entries. Evidence for gamification is thin.

### Cited Findings
- BCG lists "automatic job summary dramatically reduces manual data entry" as a core in-field AI solution, and "recording field notes and documenting job performed" as a technician activity AI should shrink. One example built "automated field notes" in-house. — [BCG 2026 PDF](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/executive-perspectives-future-of-field-service-with-applied-ai.pdf)
- ServiceTitan "Generate with TI" reads aggregated technician notes and drafts a summary that staff **review and edit before saving** (a human-in-the-loop close-out). — [ServiceTitan help](https://help.servicetitan.com/roofing/docs/manage-work-summaries-and-work-orders-for-accurate-job-records)
- Neuron7 feeds voice-captured troubleshooting steps into a "Service Decision Graph" so later technicians reuse the workaround. It can prompt when required fields are missing. Vendor-reported TransLogic case: resolution from 3 h to 3 s, 96% accuracy, 960 warranty hours saved, which I could not verify independently. — [Neuron7](https://www.neuron7.ai/blog/how-to-capture-complete-technician-activity-using-voice-in-field-service)
- MaintainX: voice notes plus AI turn what happened into a procedure available the next time the issue appears (vendor). — [MaintainX](https://www.getmaintainx.com/blog/how-to-create-maintenance-sops-with-ai-and-voice-notes)
- Expert curation: operators and managers recommended **expert or admin approval** of knowledge before it enters the base, plus conflict checks and outdated-content detection. Upkeep cost was the biggest barrier ("so many man-hours"). — [arXiv 2409.20192](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20192v1)
- Tacit-knowledge transfer works when it is embedded. In the NBER study of ~5,000 support agents, an AI assistant trained on top performers' patterns raised issues resolved per hour by **14% on average**, with the biggest gains for novices and little effect on experts. Agents with 2 months' tenure performed like 6-month agents without AI. It also reduced requests for manager intervention and improved retention. — [Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond, NBER w31161](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/w31161.pdf); [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/will-generative-ai-make-you-more-productive-work-yes-only-if-youre-not-already-great-your-job)
- Rockwell Singapore (per Microsoft): new technicians with a GenAI maintenance assistant learned to troubleshoot hundreds of machines in **3 months instead of 9** (company-reported). — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=4268797)
- Aquant positions "capturing tacit expertise of service experts" as core. Its 2026 benchmark claims up to 26% service-cost savings from scaling knowledge across the workforce (vendor). — [Aquant 2026 Benchmark (GlobeNewswire)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/02/19/3241231/0/en/Aquant-s-2026-Field-Service-Benchmark-Companies-Can-Unlock-up-to-26-in-Service-Cost-Savings-by-Scaling-Knowledge-Across-the-Workforce.html); ABB exec on the same platform: without governance "you're going to run into trouble". — [Emerj podcast](https://emerj.com/aquant-solving-workforce-workflow-challenges-in-field-services-tribal-knowledge-experts-from-aquant-abb-atlas-copco-illumina-and-tetra-pak/)
- Gamification: an academic study of a gamified industrial knowledge-sharing platform found it increased motivation, but acceptance and knowledge integration had room to improve. It proposed adapting gamification to worker profiles. I found no Augmentir evidence of gamification features or adoption metrics; Augmentir talks generically about "recognizing contributions". — [Int. J. Serious Games](https://journal.seriousgamessociety.org/index.php/IJSG/article/download/273/333/1517); [Augmentir](https://augmentir.com/product)

### Inferences
- Replace the 3 forms with one step at OT close. The AI pre-drafts "falla / causa / solución / repuestos" from what is already in the OT (tasks, consumed parts, NC text, photos, optional 20-second dictation). The mechanic confirms or corrects with one tap, and Rodrigo (jefe de taller) approves weekly into the "official" base. A "diagnostic case" is then created automatically, never as an extra form.
- Recognition should be by name and tied to the fleet ("solución aportada por Juan P., usada 4 veces"), not points or leaderboards. Evidence for gamification is weak, and leaderboards on shared tablets could feed surveillance fears.
- The novice-heavy benefit (NBER, Rockwell) suggests targeting junior and field mechanics first, and pitching the tool to seniors as "your knowledge, with your name, stays in the company", not "the AI replaces you".

### Gaps
- No independent study quantifying how much knowledge-capture volume rises when close-out moves from forms to voice or AI summary in maintenance.
- No evidence on shared-account attribution (who gets credit on a shared tablet).

## 4. Rollout practices: champions, training, measuring value, incentives

### Takeaway
Change management dominates. BCG puts field-service AI success at ~70% people and process change (versus ~30% tech) and recommends leader champions, train-the-trainer peer champions, co-designing workflows with technicians, adoption KPIs and incentives, and transparently sharing outcome improvements. Training of 5+ hours with in-person coaching correlates with regular use. Measure value with operational KPIs (first-time fix, diagnostic or repair time, repeat repairs, escalations to the jefe), not chat volume.

### Cited Findings
- BCG field service (2026): AI transformation in field service relies on change management even more than a typical digital transformation. It puts **70% on service operations change management versus 30% on building the agent tech stack** (a typical digital transformation: 70% process, 20% data/tech, 10% algorithms). Five pillars: leadership activation (leaders as adoption champions); service team engagement (**co-develop workflows with technicians**, use real-time feedback, "build trust by transparently sharing progress and improvements in service outcomes"); culture (refine KPIs, **introduce incentives to accelerate adoption**, user-level monitoring of agent performance); training (**rapid training, AI champions as multipliers via train-the-trainer**); execution (embed agents into processes such as diagnostics, with clear ownership). — [BCG 2026 PDF](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/executive-perspectives-future-of-field-service-with-applied-ai.pdf)
- BCG also advises: generic blueprints don't work; tailor to your stack and readiness; get to value fast with high-ROI use cases and prove value before scaling. — [BCG 2026 PDF](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/executive-perspectives-future-of-field-service-with-applied-ai.pdf)
- BCG AI at Work 2025: regular usage is sharply higher with **at least 5 hours of training and access to in-person training and coaching**. In secondary coverage, 79% of those with 5+ hours were regular users versus 67% with less, and 18% of regular users had received no training. — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/ai-at-work-momentum-builds-but-gaps-remain); [Unleash](https://www.unleash.ai/unleashworld/hr-tech/bcgs-ai-at-work-2025-report-four-takeaways-for-hr-leaders)
- Champions should be respected technicians, not merely early adopters (vendor guidance). Early wins appear in onboarding speed and fewer supervisor interruptions, with wrench-time gains after trust builds (vendor claim). — [MaintainX](https://www.getmaintainx.com/blog/practical-strategies-to-help-your-maintenance-team-embrace-ai)
- Metrics in use: first-time fix (industry average ~77%, leaders ~88%; the source of these benchmarks was not named). One Aquant customer reportedly went from 35% to 84% FTF after adding AI agents (single vendor example). The Geotab 2025 survey: 75% say AI improved FTF (self-reported, vendor-run). Aquant's "Avoidable Dispatch Rate" metric. — [Business Reporter](https://www.business-reporter.co.uk/improving-business-performance/how-ai-could-close-the-growing-gap-in-field-service-efficiency); [Geotab 2025](https://www.geotab.com/press-release/ai-in-field-service-2025-report/); [Aquant benchmark](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/02/19/3241231/0/en/Aquant-s-2026-Field-Service-Benchmark-Companies-Can-Unlock-up-to-26-in-Service-Cost-Savings-by-Scaling-Knowledge-Across-the-Workforce.html)
- NBER: fewer requests for managerial intervention is a measurable benefit, a proxy for "fewer calls to the jefe de taller". — [NBER w31161](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/w31161.pdf)
- MIT NANDA: success correlates with tools that adapt to the workflow and are integrated deeply. Internal builds succeed less often than partnered ones (secondary coverage). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/?mod=article_inline)

### Inferences
- For a ~10-person workshop, the rollout concretely means: Rodrigo (jefe de taller) visibly uses it and cites it in the morning meeting. Pick 1-2 respected mechanics, plus one field mechanic per mine site, as champions who co-design the OT-close step. Run short in-person sessions at the bench, not a manual. Share wins weekly ("esta semana el copiloto encontró la falla repetida del camión X").
- Success KPIs should be operational and computed from SICOM data with no extra input: repeat OT on the same patente/system within 30 days, diagnostic-to-close time, OTs closed with a captured cause/solution, and copilot cards viewed at OT open. Query count is secondary.
- Incentives can ride on the existing bono del taller only cautiously. Tying pay to AI usage counts invites gaming. Rewarding captured solutions that were later reused is more defensible, but this is untested.

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence on incentive designs for technician AI adoption.
- No published small-workshop (around 10 mechanic) rollout case; evidence is from large enterprises.

## 5. Case studies: heavy equipment, fleet, mining, Latin America

### Takeaway
Heavy-equipment cases exist (Caterpillar, Ascendum/Volvo CE dealer), but they publish little adoption data. Chilean and Peruvian mining AI is overwhelmingly predictive maintenance on haul trucks and plant, not technician copilots. No Latin American mechanic-assistant case with adoption metrics was found.

### Cited Findings
- Caterpillar Cat AI Assistant (Jan 2026, CES): voice-driven manuals, step-by-step repair guidance and parts lookup. Edge speech recognition. To be deployed across Cat.com, VisionLink, parts and service tools. Aimed partly at less experienced operators and technicians. No usage metrics published. — [Caterpillar](https://www.caterpillar.com/content/caterpillarDotCom/en/news/corporate-press-releases/h/cat-ai-assistant.html); [Global Mining Review](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/08012026/caterpillar-introduces-cat-ai-assistant/amp/)
- Ascendum (heavy-equipment dealer, Iberia): GenAI search over 13,000+ technical documents inside Salesforce. Pilot in 4 weeks. Improved first-time resolution and diagnosis (qualitative only). — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/industrials/our-insights/from-pilot-to-profit-scaling-gen-ai-in-aftermarket-and-field-services)
- X-Analytic (Chile/Peru): predictive maintenance began as a pilot at Toromocho and now monitors the full haul-truck fleet, crushers, mills and conveyors. It works with BHP (Aster), Codelco (Tantay) and Sierra Gorda. Diesel-engine failures are a frequent, costly issue. This is predictive analytics, not a mechanic copilot. — [MCH](https://www.mch.cl/minas-inteligentes-x-analytic-lleva-el-mantenimiento-predictivo-a-un-nuevo-nivel-en-chile-y-peru/)
- Chilean mining is moving toward building its own AI agents; change management and skepticism are named as the main hurdles. — [Portal Minero](https://www.portalminero.com/las-mineras-van-a-producir-sus-propias-ia-la-irrupcion-de-los-agentes-en-las-faenas-chilenas); [Reporte Minero 2024](https://www.reporteminero.cl/noticia/reportajes/2024/10/inteligencia-artificial-en-la-industria-minera)
- BCG lists fleet use cases (predictive maintenance, work-order management, compliance, TCO) as coordinated agents, and notes the trucking technician shortage costs about $2.4B a year (US). — [BCG 2026 PDF](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/executive-perspectives-future-of-field-service-with-applied-ai.pdf)

### Inferences
- SICOM has an asset most vendors lack: years of its own OT, NC, pauta, horómetro and repuestos history per patente. The documented trend (Cat, Mastertech, BCG's "summarize past incidents") is that this fleet-specific history makes answers non-generic. Lead with it.

### Gaps
- No Komatsu, Volvo Trucks, Scania or Mercedes-Benz dealer-technician GenAI adoption data was found in this search.
- No Latin American (Chile, Peru, Brazil, Mexico) case of an AI assistant for heavy-vehicle mechanics with usage metrics was found.
- The McKinsey Ascendum case page timed out, so figures beyond the search snippets were not verified.
